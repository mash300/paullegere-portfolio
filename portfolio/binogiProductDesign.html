PRODUCT DESIGN · EDTECH

Binogi

Improving lesson prioritization and writer assignment workflows for a multilingual educational content platform.

Year
2022

Role
Product Design Intern

During my internship at Binogi, I developed a Prioritizer concept to help localization supervisors identify lessons requiring attention, organize priorities, and assign writers more effectively.

I also explored improvements to FLUX, an internal tool used to track localization workflows across multiple markets.

MVP Concept 1: Prioritizer

Lesson Triage & Writer Assignment


Context

Supervisors were responsible for identifying which lessons required localization, prioritizing them by market and stage, and assigning writers accordingly.

The existing workflow required manually scanning dense datasets with limited structural guidance.

The Problem

Filtering, prioritization, and assignment were all handled within a single table view. This meant supervisors had to:

  • Mentally process large datasets
  • Interpret assignment priorities manually
  • Assign writers without clear workload visibility

This Tool exposed data, but did not support clear decision-making.

How I Approached It

I spoke directly with the Localization Supervisor to understand the workflow and where friction occurred in her day-to-day coordination.

The issue wasn’t missing features.
It was structural overload.

Breaking It Into Clearer Steps

Before redesigning anything visually, I mapped the process into stages:

  • Narrow down what needs attention
  • Review production status
  • Assign writers
  • Monitor progress

Separating these steps clarified how the interface could better support each one.

User flow for the Prioritizer concept


The MVP Concept

The Prioritizer introduced:

  • Persistent filtering by market and status
  • Clearer priority grouping
  • Inline writer assignment
  • Structured dataset refinement

The goal wasn't to simplify the work; it was to structure it more intentionally.

Outcome

The revised structure reduced ambiguity during lesson triage and made assignment decisions more straightforward.

Feedback from the supervisor indicated that the clearer grouping and filtering would make coordination less confusing and easier to manage day-to-day.

Additional Concept: FLUX

Localization Workflow Tracking


Context

FLUX was used to track localization tasks across languages and markets.

The original interface displayed over 20 columns within a single spreadsheet-style view, combining filtering, status tracking, and assignment.

The Problem

Supervisors needed to filter datasets, track production stages, assign freelancers, and monitor deadlines within the same interface.

The complexity of the work was unavoidable, but the interface amplified that complexity.

How I Approached It

I reviewed the existing tool to understand how filtering, tracking, and assignment were coupled together.

The issue wasn't a lack of information. It was how tightly everything was organized within one continuous task.

Restructuring the Workflow

I separated the workflow into clearer operational stages:

  • Refine the dataset
  • Review production status
  • Assign freelancers
  • Monitor workload and progress

This separation guided the concept.

The MVP Concept

The FLUX concept introduced:

  • Segmented task views
  • Persistent filtering panels
  • Clearer separation between status indicators and action controls
  • Structured grouping by market and language

The goal was to maintain necessary data density while reducing cognitive strain.

Outcome

The proposed structure aimed to improve scannability and clarify decision points.

When reviewing the concept, the supervisor indicated that clearer grouping and filtering would make daily coordination less confusing.

Further validation would be needed to measure improvements in efficiency and accuracy.

User flow exploring a more structured approach to localization task management.

Reflection

This project reinforced an important principle:

Complex workflows don't always need simplification; they need clearer structure. Both explorations focused on reorganizing information rather than removing it.

If developed further, I would validate improvements through measurable task completion time and error reduction.
